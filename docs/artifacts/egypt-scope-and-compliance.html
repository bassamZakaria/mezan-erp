The one finding that matters most: Egypt's B2C e-receipt regime (الإيصال الإلكتروني) is a separate mandate from e-invoicing, it is already in force, and Mezan has nothing for it. The ETA domain implements e-invoice submission only — there is no receipt document, no 72-hour post-clearance window, no QR on the printed receipt. The eighth sub-phase took effect 15 September 2025, and from 2026 every printed e-receipt must carry a QR that resolves to the validated record. Retail, restaurants and pharmacies — three of the named target buyers — cannot legally operate without it. POS being descoped does not excuse this: the obligation attaches to the sale, not to the POS product.

Two more that change the business, not just the backlog. First, the VAT and e-invoicing registration threshold dropped from EGP 500,000 to EGP 250,000 on 1 January 2026, pulling tens of thousands of micro-businesses into mandatory e-invoicing for the first time — that is Mezan's addressable market roughly doubling by decree. Second, Law 6 of 2025 gives businesses under EGP 20m turnover a turnover-based income tax of 0.4%–1.5% instead of 22.5% on profit, with simplified books and a five-year lock-in. That describes almost every Mezan prospect, and no competitor surveyed models it. An ERP that knows which regime its owner elected, and keeps the books that regime requires, is a differentiator nobody has claimed yet.

What actually wins Egypt is four things in order: compliance that holds on audit day, Arabic that reads as accounting Arabic rather than translation, running with no internet, and a price a shop owner can pay once. Three of those four are already Mezan's, and no competitor in the matrix has all four — Daftra and SMACC are cloud-only, Odoo and SAP need an implementation partner, QuickBooks has no Arabic at all.

The screen work is unchanged in shape and smaller in scale than the first draft claimed: 115 nav entries become 71 by cutting 11 and merging 18, then 80 once the 9 Egypt-mandated surfaces are built. Three cuts from the first draft are reversed by the Egypt test — promotions, notification rules, and one CRM surface — because an Egyptian buyer compares Mezan to Daftra and to the local reference product, never to QuickBooks.

Section 01

What wins Egypt

In short

Four axes, in priority order: compliance, Arabic, offline, price. Feature count is not on the list. Every competitor in the matrix beats Mezan on breadth; none of them holds all four axes, and the four are what a shop owner actually buys on.

Compliance that holds
Axis 1 · non-negotiable

An ERP in Egypt is judged the day the tax inspector arrives. E-invoice, e-receipt, VAT return, Form 41, five-year retention, an audit trail nobody can edit. Failure here is not a lost feature — it is a fine and a lost customer.

Section 02 audits all fourteen obligations against the code.

Arabic as the first language
Axis 2 · the trust test

المحاسب is trained on مدين / دائن, on سند قبض, on مستخلص. A product that translates English concepts into Arabic words reads as foreign in the first thirty seconds.

RTL by construction, enforced by pnpm lint:rtl. This is already a moat and it is the one QuickBooks structurally cannot copy.

Runs with no internet
Axis 3 · the structural differentiator

Every cloud competitor stops when the line drops. Egyptian shops outside the main corridors lose connectivity routinely, and a till that cannot ring a sale is a closed shop.

Electron + SQLite, LAN multi-till, offline Ed25519 licence. Only SAP Business One among the surveyed products offers true on-premises, at 100× the price.

A price an owner can pay
Axis 4 · the close

صاحب المنشأة signs the cheque. Daftra runs EGP 489–1,960/month, the local reference from EGP 1,500/month plus per-module add-ons, both forever. A perpetual offline licence is a different purchase decision, not a cheaper one.

The order matters. A product that is beautiful, fast and complete but cannot issue a compliant e-receipt is unsellable to a retailer; a product that is compliant, Arabic and offline but plain will still close. Spend on the axes in order, and treat every feature request as a question about which axis it serves.

The five named users are the test for each: المُدخِل اليومي keying 40–80 invoices a day, أمين المخزن on one screen all day, المحاسب closing the month, صاحب المنشأة opening it twice a week and signing the cheque, and الدعم solving it by phone. A decision that serves only the first loses the sale.

Section 03

The three gaps

In short

E-receipt, Form 41, and the Law 6/2025 regime. The first blocks three named target segments outright; the second fails an audit; the third is the largest uncontested differentiator available in this market. Everything else in the backlog is smaller than these.

Gap 1 · The B2C e-receipt الإيصال الإلكتروني

Blocks three named segments

The e-receipt regime is not the e-invoice regime with a different name. It is a separate ETA system, phased separately, with different mechanics: post-clearance rather than pre-clearance, a 72-hour submission window rather than immediate, and from 2026 a mandatory QR on the printed receipt that resolves to the validated record. Reprinting must reproduce the same QR, never mint a new one.

Mezan implements the e-invoice path and nothing else. The consequence is concrete: a retailer, a restaurant or a pharmacy running Mezan is not compliant on a consumer sale. client-retail, client-restaurant and client-pharmacy are all named target buyers, so this is not a future segment — it is three of the ones already being designed for.

"POS is descoped" does not resolve it. The obligation attaches to the sale, not to the POS product, and a sales invoice raised at a counter to a walk-in consumer is a B2C transaction whatever screen raised it. The minimum honest build is: a receipt submission type in the ETA domain, the 72-hour queue-and-forward behaviour the desktop edition already uses for invoices, a QR on the printed receipt, and a reprint that is idempotent.

The offline architecture is an advantage here, not an obstacle. A 72-hour window is exactly what queue-and-forward is for — the shop rings the sale with no internet, the receipt prints, and the submission goes when the line returns. A cloud competitor with a dropped connection simply cannot ring the sale. Say this out loud in the sales conversation.

Gap 2 · Form 41, the quarterly withholding return

The withholding mechanics are correct and well-built — the supplier is discharged in full while only the net leaves the treasury, which is the part most systems get wrong. What is missing is the return itself: a quarterly period, a filing record, a remittance figure, and a lock that refuses a backdated posting into a filed quarter.

The design already exists in the codebase. tax/vat-filing.ts solves precisely this problem for VAT, and its own header explains why: before it, "July's output tax moved by 140.00 after the fact, with no amendment, no warning and no trace". Form 41 needs the same object with a quarterly window. That makes it a small, well-specified build rather than a new domain.

Gap 3 · The Law 6/2025 regime — the uncontested differentiator

Nobody else models this

Turnover under EGP 20m, income tax at 0.4%–1.5% of turnover instead of 22.5% of profit, exemptions from stamp duty and dividend and capital-gains tax, simplified books, and a five-year lock-in on the election. That is a description of the Mezan customer. None of Daftra, SMACC, AccFlex, Odoo, Zoho or the local reference product markets support for it.

What it needs is small relative to what it buys: a regime field on company settings, the turnover bands, a computation that reports the liability from figures the ledger already holds, and — the part that actually sells — a comparison: what this business would pay under each regime, from its own numbers. An owner deciding whether to elect a regime they cannot leave for five years will pay for that screen.

It also has a compliance consequence worth catching: the election is irrevocable for five years, so the regime is a fact about a period, not a setting someone toggles. Model it the way vat-filing models a filed period — a claim about a window, with an explicit amendment path — not as a boolean in settings.

Section 04

The 2026 market shift

In short

Two decrees moved the market in Mezan's favour and neither is in the plan. The registration threshold fell to EGP 250,000, forcing tens of thousands of micro-businesses into e-invoicing for the first time; Law 6/2025 gave those same businesses a reason to formalise. The buyer being created is a shop with no accountant, no IT, and now a legal obligation.

That argues for a cheaper entry tier, a faster setup, and a ruthlessly short path from install to first compliant invoice — and it argues against every screen the product does not need.

Features that make offline better — a bulletproof backup, a fast restore, a same-QR reprint, a 72-hour submission queue that survives a week without a line — are the product.

Section 07

Where QuickBooks fits

In short

Demoted, and still useful. QuickBooks answers exactly one question well — how few screens can carry this? — and it answers it better than any product in the Egyptian set, all of which grew by module accretion. It sets no scope, arbitrates no behaviour, and supplies no Arabic.

The three-question rule stands, with the order corrected so Egypt goes first:

  1. Does Egyptian law require it? If yes, it is built, at whatever cost. Section 02 is the list.
  2. Does the competitive floor require it? If yes, it is built at the shallowest depth that survives a comparison meeting. Section 06.
  3. Would QuickBooks build it, and how thinly? This does not decide whether. It decides how small.

Odoo stays the control: a feature Odoo ships, QuickBooks lacks, and neither Egyptian law nor the competitive floor requires was copied from an app list rather than requested by a customer. That test is what produces the 11 cuts.

What QuickBooks is genuinely worth copying

The +New menu
Highest value

One button on every screen opens every document a user can create, grouped by who it concerns. You never navigate in order to create. Mezan's nearest equivalent is a Ctrl+K palette that صاحب المنشأة will never discover.

One list, a type column
Information architecture

Products and services is a single list with Type as a column and a filter. Six Mezan item screens collapse into it. The same move fixes treasury's 11 voucher types.

The report centre
Biggest single win

~80 reports, no report screens — one centre with favourites, a customise drawer, scheduling, and drill-down to the transaction from every figure. Mezan has 22 identical tables with no drill-down, which is المحاسب's stated breaking point.

Setup as you go
Now urgent

Three questions, then a working file, with the rest surfaced as dashboard tasks. Against a buyer newly dragged over the EGP 250,000 threshold with no accountant, time-to-first-compliant-invoice is the metric that closes the sale.

Where following QuickBooks would break the product

Settings. QuickBooks hides policy that Egyptian operations need: credit-control mode, backorders, negative stock, require-PO-before-GRN, require-GRN-before-invoice, negative-cash block, tax-inclusive pricing, missing-exchange-rate policy (R11.1–R11.8). These toggles are what make an ERP an ERP rather than a ledger. Keep all ten tabs; QuickBooks is simply worse here.

The bank-feed loop. QuickBooks Online's daily rhythm is reviewing bank-feed transactions, and most of its dashboard exists to serve that. Mezan is offline and has no feed. Copying that visual language yields a dashboard with nothing to review.

One account for receivables. QuickBooks has no sub-ledger/control-account split. Mezan's ledger invariants require agreement between them, and D1 already records what happens when two stores of the same fact disagree. The heavier design is the correct one.

The vocabulary, uncritically. Adopting assembly was right — a plainer word for the same thing. Adopting Credit memo for مرتجع مبيعات or Class for مركز تكلفة would be wrong. Copy the plainness, not the words; QuickBooks has no Arabic to borrow.

The prerequisite nobody has met

Every QuickBooks claim in this brief comes from published Intuit documentation, not from a live tenant. Before the cut list is executed, run the site-feature-audit method against an Intuit Developer sandbox or an Online trial and produce docs/quickbooks-baseline/ — the same four artifacts the reference audit produced. Note also that "QuickBooks" is two products: 13 of the surviving screens are justified by Desktop Enterprise (assemblies, multi-site transfers, price levels, landed cost, barcode, physical count, sales-by-rep, fixed-asset manager) and by nothing in Online.

Section 09

The plan

In short

Compliance first, then the cut, then the patterns. The e-receipt gap outranks everything in the backlog, including the QuickBooks audit — a screen count is a preference, and an unissued legal receipt is a fine.

  1. Close the e-receipt gap

    A receipt submission type in the ETA domain, the 72-hour post-clearance window, queue-and-forward on the existing offline path, a QR on the printed receipt that resolves to the validated record, and a reprint that reproduces the same QR rather than minting a new one. Verify against the ETA's own sandbox before claiming it.

    P0 · blocks retail, F&B, pharmacy · 8–12 days
  2. Wire the e-seal, or say plainly that it is test-mode

    service.ts notes the signing agent is not connected. Production document version 1.0 needs it. Until it is wired, the product is test-mode only and that must be visible in the UI rather than discovered by a customer at an audit.

    P0 · 5–8 days
  3. Build the Form 41 return

    Clone the vat-filing.ts shape with a quarterly window: a filing record, the remitted figure, the authority's reference, and a refusal of backdated postings into a filed quarter unless explicitly amended.

    P0 · 4–6 days
  4. Verify the printed invoice and the statements

    Print one real invoice and one full set of financial statements. Check the invoice field-by-field against a compliant sample; have a محاسب قانوني sign off the statements as EAS-conformant. Both are cheap and neither is proven today.

    P0 · 2–3 days
  5. Build the tax regime and Law 6/2025

    Regime as a fact about a period rather than a settings boolean, the turnover bands, the liability computation, and the comparison screen that shows an owner what they would pay each way. The largest uncontested differentiator on the board, and it will not stay uncontested.

    P1 · 8–10 days
  6. Run the QuickBooks baseline audit

    Answer "which QuickBooks" and record it as DEC-8; obtain an Intuit Developer sandbox or an Online trial; produce docs/quickbooks-baseline/ with the same four artifacts the reference audit produced. Then re-tag the ~90 requirements by provenance — remembering that provenance and validity are independent, so R1.1's server-side counts survive whoever wrote them.

    P1 · 6–8 days
  7. Execute the cuts and merges

    11 cuts, 3 reductions, 18 merges. Hide before deleting, per ae17929, so the change is reversible for one release. Start with items 6 → 1 and stock 14 → 9.

    P2 · 8–12 days
  8. Build the remaining ETA surfaces and account routing

    Readiness meter, credentials with test-connection, item mapping, unit mapping (R7.1–R7.6), plus account routing (R6.1) and customer/supplier statements (R3.6).

    P2 · 10–14 days
  9. Build the +New menu and the report centre

    The two QuickBooks patterns worth more than every cut combined. The report centre also fixes reports quarantined from their data, which is المحاسب's stated breaking point.

    P3 · 12–18 days
  10. Shorten time-to-first-compliant-invoice

    Against a buyer newly dragged over the EGP 250,000 threshold with no accountant, this is the metric that closes the sale. Setup-as-you-go rather than a gating wizard; a seeded Egyptian chart of accounts; VAT and withholding rates pre-configured; the ETA connection tested during setup.

    P3 · 6–10 days

Roughly three weeks of compliance work before anything else, then six to ten weeks of scope and pattern work. Nothing here touches HR, which stays deferred with its statutory numbers recorded for when it returns.
