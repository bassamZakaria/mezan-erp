Mezan ERP · Design Plan

What we're about to change, drawn

Nine design decisions left open by the audit. Each one is a figure showing the actual difference between the choices — not a restated list — followed by the options themselves. Pick one per decision; the bar at the bottom collects them.

Decision 01 · The size system

How big is a control?

Nine different sizes are in play today and eight of them are below the touch minimum. The question isn't whether to raise them — it's whether one number governs everything, or two.

Decision 02 · Numerals

Why the label and the box disagree

This is a wiring diagram, not a preference. Display figures pass through the tenant's numeral setting; input figures skip it entirely and hit a hardcoded default.

Decision 03 · Acting on a row

Where does "do something to this invoice" live?

Today the answer is: behind a hover, on a 28px target, on 64 screens. Three ways out, and they differ in what the screen looks like when you are not pointing at anything.

Decision 04 · Focus in overlays

Where the Tab key goes when a panel is open

Three overlays declare themselves modal and behave as if they aren't. This is the mechanism, and it is the same one in all three.

Decision 05 · Reading a chart

Mapping a wedge to its name

Ten expense categories, ten hues, and a legend the eye has to colour-match against. One in twelve men cannot make some of those matches at all.

Decision 06 · What a screen says back

Every state a screen can be in

A screen is a small state machine. Four of its states existed nowhere in the product a day ago; two still have no agreed shape.

Decision 07 · Where a document is written

430 pixels, or a page

62 screens edit inside a fixed-width slide-over. Drawn to scale against the window it sits in, the problem is not subtle.

Decision 08 · What comes out of the printer

The one artefact the customer keeps

The receipt config is fully editable and read by no renderer. Deciding what to build means deciding what the printed page owes an Egyptian buyer.

Decision 09 · The signature

One component, five places it's the same truth

The product is called ميزان. Double-entry, a cash close, a reconciliation and a stock count are all the same shape: two sides that must agree, and a difference to explain when they don't.

My set: 1B · 2A · 3B · 4A · 5B · 6A · 7B · 8A · 9A. Two tiers of control size rather than one, inputs that read the same numeral setting the labels do, a row control that exists before you point at it, one focus hook shared by every overlay, ranked bars instead of a ten-hue donut, refusals that name the next action, documents on real pages, a receipt renderer that actually consumes its own settings, and the balance built once and used five times.

The two I would expect you to push back on are 5B — replacing the donut is the most visible change on the dashboard — and 8B versus 8A, which is really a question about whether you are selling to retail shops with thermal printers this year.
