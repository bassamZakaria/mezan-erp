Mezan ERP · Element Audit

Every element, judged against the person who uses it

Fifty-six elements — every primitive, shell part, overlay and print component the product renders through — audited across four lenses: visual design, information design, accessibility, and the everyday operator. Every finding cites the line it was read from, and the load-bearing ones were re-verified by hand.

What the audit actually found

Thirty-odd findings, seven causes

Almost nothing here is a one-off mistake. The individual findings collapse into a handful of root causes, and fixing a cause fixes every element downstream of it — which is why the fix plan is much shorter than the finding list.

One constant sets nearly every control below the touch minimum

40 of 48 controls on one screen

CONTROL_SIZE.md is min-h-[38px] (form.tsx:20-23), and it is the base that TextInput, Select and SearchInput all inherit. Around it sit independently-sized controls that never got the memo: Button md ≈36px, IconButton 37×37, ChromeIconButton 36×36 (which backs five topbar controls), RowMenu 28×28, QtyInput steppers 26×26, Checkbox 17×17, AccountTree's expander 18×18. Measured live: 40 of 48 controls on the invoice list and 27 of 34 on the dashboard are under 44px.

Fix: raise the shared constant, then sweep the eight controls that size themselves independently. One change carries most of the app; the sweep is mechanical.

Three overlays claim to be modal and aren't

62 screens

SidePanel sets role="dialog" aria-modal="true" but never moves focus into itself, never traps Tab, and never hides the page behind it from a screen reader (SidePanel.tsx:41-53). CommandPalette and NotificationsPanel have the same shape of gap. A keyboard user tabs straight out of an open dialog into the dimmed page underneath.

Fix: ConfirmDialog — built yesterday — already does all three correctly, including focusing Cancel rather than Confirm on destructive actions. Copy it into the other three. This is not new design work.

The numeral system splits down the middle of every form

Every money and quantity field

Read-only figures go through useFormat() and honour the tenant's chosen numeral system. The inputs do not: MoneyInput calls formatMoney(value) bare (MoneyInput.tsx:47) and QtyInput renders String(value) (QtyInput.tsx:75), while formatNumber defaults to numerals = "western" (lib/format.ts:40). Pick Arabic-Indic numerals and the label reads ٤٤٬٢٠٠ while the box beneath it reads 44,200. Live, the dashboard already has 15 components mixing both systems.

Fix: route both inputs through the shared numerals context, the same way Money and Qty already do.

The busiest control in the product is invisible until you hover

64 list screens

The row-action cell in DocumentList is opacity-0 until group-hover/row (DocumentList.tsx:461). It is the only hover-only reveal in the entire codebase — and it is on every row of every list. Underneath it sits a 28×28px RowMenu trigger, so the control is both hidden and tiny.

Fix: one line. Give the cell a resting visible state instead of zero opacity, and grow the trigger.

Charts encode meaning in hue alone — and one drops a minus sign

Reports and dashboard

DonutChart wedges and legend swatches are identically-shaped colour dots (charts.tsx:283-307); TrendChart separates series by stroke colour only (charts.tsx:150-187). Worse than styling: ReportChart's pie view feeds Math.abs(point.value) into the donut (ReportChart.tsx:121), so a refund renders identical to a sale — the same figure the bar and table views show correctly with its sign.

Fix: the sign-dropping is a correctness bug, not a design one — fix it first. Then give each series a shape, dash or marker so identity survives without colour.

Copy that describes the codebase, on the product surface

4 architecture strings, 6 state diagrams

The sales-invoice subtitle naming its own service class was one instance of a habit. Still shipping: «سجل القيود المحاسبية — مسودة ← بانتظار الموافقة ← مرحل ← معكوس، على المحرك المحاسبي الحقيقي» and «سندات القبض والصرف والتحويل — مستند موحّد بـ11 نوعاً على إطار المستندات». Refusals state the rule without the remedy: «لا يمكن تسجيل دفعة في حالة الفاتورة الحالية» never says "confirm the invoice first". And (queued) sits in English inside an Arabic sentence.

Fix: rewrite ten strings. A page subtitle says what the screen is for; a refusal says what to do next.

The POS receipt is fully configurable and wired to nothing

Silent

posReceipt is defined in types.ts:103,152 and fully editable in Settings — title, tax number, customer name, thank-you text (PrintTemplatesPage.tsx:321-324). No renderer reads it. A shop owner configures their receipt and nothing they change ever reaches paper. Alongside it: printed invoices carry no e-invoice QR or UUID, the company logo loads from a remote URL in an offline-first product, and printToPdf has no .catch, so a failed save is silent.

Fix: build the narrow-receipt renderer that consumes the config, or remove the settings tab. Shipping a control that does nothing is worse than not shipping it.

What came back clean

19 elements, no findings

No colour literals anywhere — every utility resolves to a real token in both themes. No page scrolls sideways at 125% zoom. Wide tables scroll inside their own container with a sticky header and a sticky totals row. DisclosureChevron mirrors via [dir="rtl"] rather than a hardcoded transform. Kbd isolates Latin so an Arabic screen reader doesn't try to pronounce "Ctrl+K". StatusPill pairs a dot with a word. ConfirmDialog is a model implementation.

Read this as: the foundations are sound. What's wrong is concentrated and mechanical, not architectural.

The plan

Four waves, cheapest leverage first

Ordered by how much of the product each wave moves per unit of work, not by where the findings happened to be listed.

Wave 1 is roughly a day and repairs the majority of the P1 list, because almost all of it lives in shared primitives rather than in the ~90 screens that render through them.
Wave 1 · Touch

Raise the size floor once, then sweep the strays

CONTROL_SIZE.md to 44px, which carries TextInput, Select and SearchInput with it. Then the eight controls that size themselves: Button, IconButton, ChromeIconButton, RowMenu, QtyInput steppers, Checkbox, AccountTree's expander, DateRangeFilter presets — plus my own pin button, which is 44 tall and 36 wide and fails the rule I wrote.

Moves: every screen in the product, in one commit.

Wave 2 · Truth

Fix what misstates a number or hides a control

The Math.abs sign-drop in the pie view. The numerals split between display and input. The hover-hidden row action. These are correctness, not polish — a report that quietly turns a refund into a sale costs more trust than every styling defect on this page combined.

Moves: 64 list screens and every report with a pie view.

Wave 3 · Focus

Make the three fake modals real

Port ConfirmDialog's focus trap, focus-on-open and focus-restore into SidePanel, CommandPalette and NotificationsPanel. Give TopbarMenu either real arrow-key menu semantics or an honest non-menu role, and add tabIndex={-1} to combobox options so Tab stops walking into the listbox.

Moves: 62 drawer screens plus the whole shell, for keyboard-first data entry.

Wave 4 · Words & paper

Say what the screen does; make the printer honest

Rewrite the ten worst strings — subtitles that describe the code, refusals with no remedy, the English leaking into Arabic. Then print: build the receipt renderer that consumes posReceipt or drop the settings tab, embed the logo as a data URI so it survives offline, add the e-invoice QR, and catch the PDF failure instead of swallowing it.

Moves: the first impression, and the one artefact the customer keeps.

The headline is not that 37 elements need work. It is that 19 came back clean, no element needs replacing, and every remaining defect traces to seven causes — one shared constant, one copied focus pattern, one formatting context, one line of CSS, one Math.abs, ten strings, and one config object nothing reads.

That is a good result for a product this size. It means the work ahead is a sweep, not a rebuild — and that the ground rules can be enforced by review from here on, because there is nothing structural standing in their way.
